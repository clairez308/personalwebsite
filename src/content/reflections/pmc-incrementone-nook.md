Our team just won the pitch competition at the PMC x incrementOne program at UBC! 🥳 We built Nook, a friends-only map for UBC students. You log places you've been with a quick verdict and a photo or short video, so your friends can check whether a spot they saw online is actually worth going to. Then you can turn a saved place into a group plan, pick a day, and lock it in once enough people say "I'm in."

What I'm most proud of is how we got there. We didn't start with the app. We started by talking to students! Seven of the eight we interviewed found the last new place they tried through another person, not a star rating. Of the six who named a barrier to trying somewhere new, four said it was having nobody to go with, or friends cancelling.

Those two problems, trust and follow-through, became our focus. Google Maps has reviews, but not from your friends. Beli has friends' ratings, but only for restaurants. That gap is where Nook fits!

A huge thank you to Chris Hobbs, our mentor, for the time and guidance, and for writing about our work. He pointed out that AI tools make building faster every month, but knowing what to build and for whom is still the hard part. Thank you also to Chloë's exec dev team for hosting the event, and to Dave S. and Kevin Lindsay for facilitating the program.

Huge congrats to my team, Chloë Dacey, Dayson How, Jay Jay Silapakalnpradit and Roberta Lee! We spent a week learning a lot together, and I'm so grateful for it.

**Key learning:** Talk to your users before you build. The answer was in what people actually do, not in what the app could do.
